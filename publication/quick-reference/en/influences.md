# Influences and Transformations

Influences and transformations do not replace a Worldview. They modify its reading or durably transform the Protagonist's relationship to the Cosmos.

## Lunar Influence

Lunar influence applies only when the Protagonist's relationship to the Moon is **relevant to the resolution**.

### Beyond the Glowline

#### Full Moon

- **Theism**: `1`s may be rerolled.
- **Animism**: two `1`s forming a Spiritual Pact exceptionally produce **2 Successes** instead of one.
- **Logic**: add **1 to the value of every die** before calculating the total.

#### Half Moons and Crescents

Use the **ordinary** reading of the Worldview.

#### Dying Moon and Black Moon

The affected Worldview enters its **Weakened Worldview**.

### Within the Glowline

When Lunar influence is relevant, use the **ordinary reading** of the Worldview regardless of the visible phase.

### Unaffected Worldviews

**Mysticism** and **Draconic Thought** are not modified by the Lunar cycle.

## Illumination

### Spark

An experience that cracks a Worldview without producing full Illumination may leave a **Trace**: doubt, fascination, incomplete revelation, or an impossible memory that cannot be forgotten.

The Trace grants no automatic power. It may become a Bet when relevant.

### Shift

Illumination must first be made genuinely possible by the fiction: riddle-master, Sevening, Illuminated teaching, mystical experience, an appropriate Heroquest, draconic confrontation, or another comparable Situation.

After both rolls have been read normally, the Protagonist may then **play one of their `1`s**:

- the `1` removes one `6` from the opposing roll;
- both dice disappear together at comparison;
- the Protagonist becomes **Illuminated**.

The shift is irreversible.

### After Illumination

The Protagonist retains their Worldview and its usual rules.

After both rolls have been read normally, **each of their `1`s may remove one opposing `6`**, with both dice disappearing together at comparison.

## Chaos

Chaos may be invoked only if the act itself opens a breach: transgression, destruction, cruelty, corruption, or an unnatural transformation.

### First Pact

The first time a Protagonist gives in to Chaos to overcome an obstacle or escape a certain outcome:

- they immediately gain a **Feat**;
- no dice are rolled for that resolution;
- they receive a **Taint**, described by a new Chaotic Keyword.

### Calling on Chaos again

After the first pact, Chaos may force a resolution, up to ignoring a Frame Factor when the fiction genuinely permits that rupture.

Each new invocation requires permanently crossing out something that still bound the Protagonist to the Cosmos:

- a **positive Keyword**;
- a **Runic Affinity**;
- a **Bond**;
- an **important Attachment**.

The loss must exist in the fiction.

### Growth of the Taint

Each new use may cause the Taint to evolve:

1. **First sign** — subtle, almost benign, but unnatural.
2. **The sign asserts itself** — visible and constraining.
3. **The hold grows** — the Taint invades daily life.
4. **Total hold** — it leaves almost no room for what the Protagonist once was.

The Taint may become a Bet when it genuinely matters in a Situation.

### Downfall

When nothing remains that the Protagonist can sacrifice, Chaos no longer grants this exceptional power: the transformation has won.

A Chaotic creature or god normally uses its own Worldview. Chaos does not automatically add Successes to everything Chaotic.
