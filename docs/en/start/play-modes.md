# Playing Together

Glorantha Perspectives rests on two simple functions: **carrying a Protagonist** and **carrying Time**.

These functions say who makes the decisions that move play forward. They are not classes of player and do not impose a single table structure.

## Carrying a Protagonist

To carry a **Protagonist** is to decide what they want, what they attempt, what they say, and how they react to what they experience.

The player carrying them does not decide what the world grants them. They choose the Protagonist's intention and act from what the Protagonist knows, believes, owns, and feels.

## Carrying Time

To carry **Time** is to make Glorantha live around the Protagonists: present the Situation, act for people and powers that are not carried as Protagonists, advance what keeps moving without them, answer their choices, and maintain the coherence of the world.

Time does not decide the future of the Protagonists and is not there to defeat them.

When a resolution needs arbitration over the Focus, a Stake, or a Frame Factor, the table first compares the proposals with what is true in the fiction. If disagreement remains, the person carrying Time makes the final call when the table agreement gives them that responsibility.

## The simplest way to begin

For a first game, use the familiar roleplaying arrangement:

- each player usually carries **one Protagonist**;
- another person mainly carries **Time**.

In other words, the first players carry the player characters and the other person takes the role usually called the **game master**.

If you already know roleplaying games, you can start this way without learning a new table structure. Glorantha Perspectives works immediately with this distribution.

## Other forms of play are possible

Glorantha Perspectives does not, however, depend on that distribution. A table may experiment with other ways of carrying Protagonists and Time if it wants to:

- rotate Time from one Situation to another;
- share some responsibilities of Time;
- let one player carry several Protagonists;
- share a Protagonist;
- play solo, using Runes, oracles, tables, Glorantha sources, or an AI to preserve discovery and uncertainty;
- invent another arrangement suited to the game.

These are not **official modes** you need to learn before play. They are possibilities made available because the rules act on the fiction rather than on powers reserved to a particular role at the table.

An arrangement works as long as everyone knows **what they may decide, what they do not decide, and how the table settles disagreement**.

## Using GP inside another game

Glorantha Perspectives can also be used occasionally during a campaign played with **RuneQuest** or another system.

When the interesting question becomes less “which skill should we roll?” and more **“what really weighs on this Situation?”**, the table can pause the usual system, state the Focus, build the Stakes, and resolve that question with GP.

Nothing needs to be converted. A high skill, a Passion, a Rune, a reputation, a cult, an injury, or an object can simply be recognized as a fictional truth and become a Stake when relevant. **Numerical values never become numbers of dice.**

One handoff rule is essential:

> **Never resolve the same question twice.**

If GP establishes that the Protagonists cross the Lunar lines but one of them emerges badly wounded, the host system resumes from that new fiction. It may express the injury, spent resources, or local consequences mechanically; it does not roll again to decide whether the group crossed the lines after all.
