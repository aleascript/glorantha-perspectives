# Glorantha at a Glance

You do not need to know all of Glorantha before you start playing. A few landmarks are enough to understand what the Protagonists see around them and why some things immediately matter in a Situation.

This page is not meant to summarize Glorantha. Instead, it offers a **compass for your attention**: a few places to look when you first encounter a Situation.

## A compass for looking at Glorantha

When you do not yet know what is important, look first at:

- **community**: who do these people belong with, whom do they owe duties to, and whom can they rely on?
- **Runes**: what forces, affinities, or tensions of the world can be recognized in the people, places, and events before you?
- **gods, spirits, and magic**: what powers are present, invoked, honored, feared, or simply taken for granted?
- **bonds and obligations**: who loves whom, who owes what to whom, and what oaths, debts, rivalries, or responsibilities are involved?
- **culture**: what do these people consider normal, honorable, shameful, sacred, or impossible?
- **place**: what makes this location particular, and what stories, powers, or communities are attached to it?
- **myth**: does what is happening now echo something that already happened in God Time?
- **Worldview**: what kind of world do these people think they live in, and how do they understand what is happening to them?

This is not a checklist to run through in every scene. These are directions for your attention. A Situation becomes Gloranthan as soon as a few of these things begin to matter to the people living through it.

## You belong to a community

Completely isolated individuals are rare. A Protagonist usually belongs to a family, clan, tribe, city, temple, regiment, school, tradition, or another community that gives them obligations as well as support.

These ties are not background decoration: they can become Bets as soon as they genuinely matter in the Situation.

The same is true of opponents: an ordinary enemy is still someone. They too belong to communities, honor powers, carry obligations, and have a place in the Cosmos. Conflict may be violent without automatically reducing the other to something that exists only to be destroyed.

## The world evokes the Bronze Age

Weapons, tools, buildings, roads, forms of power, and exchange belong to societies very different from modern ones. Some are tribal, others urban or imperial; each has its own customs and its own sense of what is normal.

This visual landmark does not define Gloranthan cultures by itself, but it helps avoid instinctively projecting modern institutions, values, or habits onto them.

## Runes are everywhere

Runes are not merely magical signs. They appear in tattoos, clothing, weapons, temples, objects, landscapes, and ways of thinking.

They can also mark a Protagonist's character and provide a shared language for understanding what runs through a Situation.

When a Rune is visible or felt, ask less “what bonus does it give?” than **“what does it mean here?”**

## Magic can be seen

Magic is usually not invisible or abstract. It can alter air, light, sound, bodies, objects, or the elements of a place. When it acts, the world shows it.

Magic is therefore part of what characters can observe, interpret, fear, hope for, and connect to their beliefs.

## Places have memory

A river, mountain, temple, ruin, or wood is not necessarily mere scenery. Communities, powers, and myths may be attached to it.

When a place becomes important, ask what happened there, who claims it, who inhabits it, who is honored there, and what people do differently there than elsewhere.

## Myth remains close

In Glorantha, the mythic past is not merely ancient history. The deeds of gods and heroes in God Time still shape the world, rituals, and ways of acting.

You do not need to know every myth. When a resemblance becomes meaningful, it is already enough to direct your attention: **is what is happening now replaying something older?**

## Chaos breaks the order of the world

**Chaos** is not simply the name given to the most dangerous enemies. It is a rupture in the distinctions, relationships, and constraints that make the world readable.

Where an ordinary opponent still has a place in the Cosmos, Chaos can call into question what the Protagonists thought possible or stable. Its presence is recognized less by cruelty than by the way it distorts reality itself.

See [Chaos](../perspectives/chaos/index.md) for how this rupture can become an Influence in Glorantha Perspectives.

## Beliefs change how people act

A theist, animist, logician, or mystic does not merely describe magic with different words. They live in a world they understand differently.

In Glorantha Perspectives, this **Worldview** directly changes how dice are read when an outcome becomes uncertain.

Learning a foreign rite, text, or practice is not enough to change Worldview. A true change reflects a deep transformation in how the Protagonist understands reality.

## The world is colorful

Glorantha is often vivid. Colors may carry cultural, religious, or runic meaning. Skin, hair, eyes, clothes, and objects can bear marks that would seem extraordinary in our world and perfectly ordinary here.

## Exchange depends on communities

Barter and small coinage remain important in everyday life. High-value currencies are often tied to cities, temples, cults, or authorities that guarantee exchange.

See [Currency and Exchange](../time/currency.md) for a few practical landmarks.

## Healing does not erase everything

Healing magic can save a life and speed recovery. It does not necessarily make wounds consequence-free or simply erase everything that happened to the body.

What remains after an injury may continue to matter in later Situations.

## There is always more to discover

Glorantha is immense. When a detail becomes important, consult the sources that concern the people, place, cult, or event involved.

You do not need to know everything before playing. **First learn what to look at.** Play will then give you reasons to look further.
