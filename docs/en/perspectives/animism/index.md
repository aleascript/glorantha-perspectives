## Inhabiting the world

The animist inhabits a populated world.

A stream, a hill, a sickness, an animal, a weapon, or a wind may have a presence that must be reckoned with.

Spirits are not an invisible army waiting for orders. Some are familiar, others foreign. Some answer spontaneously. Some must be recognized, persuaded, appeased, or bound.

The animist therefore does not merely ask: "what can help me?" They ask: **"who is here, and what relationship is possible now?"**

*Others readily call them primitives or savages.*

## Reading the roll

- Each **even** die counts as a Success: a spirit answers.
- Two dice showing the **same odd value** form a **Spiritual Pact** and together produce one Success.
- A lone odd die has no effect: something was present, but no useful relationship could be established.

A decisive outcome may turn a momentary relationship into a lasting one:

- an **Exploit** may allow a spirit to be bound into a fetish;
- a **Fiasco** may cause a bound spirit to depart.

## When the Worldview falters or is fulfilled

### Unravelled Bonds — Weakened Worldview

Even dice still produce their Successes. Spirits still exist, and some still answer.

But matching odd dice can no longer form a Spiritual Pact.

The problem is not the absence of the spirit world. It is the inability to establish a new relationship with it.

### The Circle of Spirits — Heroic Worldview

If **the lowest and highest dice in the roll share the same parity**, every Bet succeeds.

The extremes answer one another. What seemed like a collection of independent presences suddenly appears as an attuned whole.

It is no longer one spirit answering, or even one particular pact.

**The whole circle has closed around the action.**

## Where to look

Even dice give their Successes immediately.

But your attention should go especially to **odd dice**. Look for those that resemble one another: *who has just recognized whom?*

In the Heroic Worldview, widen your gaze to the two extremes: *do the humblest and the greatest presences belong to the same movement?*

## Example of play

A shaman seeks a path across a marsh before nightfall.

She rolls:

`3 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 5`

The `4` answers directly: one Success. The two `3`s form a first **Spiritual Pact**. The two `5`s form another.

The roll therefore gives three Successes.

In the fiction, this does not necessarily mean that three spirits appear before her. The shaman may feel the marsh itself arranging around her passage: insects change direction, a sheet of water withdraws, reeds reveal an old path.

She has not merely found a route. She understands which presences of the place have momentarily come into accord with her.

> **DESIGN — Why this reading?**
>
> Animism does not reduce odd dice to failed results. They represent a spirit world that is present but difficult to bring into accord.
>
> The Spiritual Pact makes a Success emerge from the **relationship between two presences** rather than from an isolated face.
>
> The Circle of Spirits carries that logic to fulfillment: when the extremes themselves are attuned, the whole roll can be read as one coherent relationship.
