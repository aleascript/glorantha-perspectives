# Worldviews

In **Glorantha Perspectives**, a **Worldview** is neither a profession, an intellectual method, nor the kind of magic being used at that moment. It represents a **lived ontology**: the deep and spontaneous way in which the Protagonist thinks the world works.

> **A Worldview does not describe what you do. It describes the world in which you think you are doing it.**

When reality must answer an uncertain Situation, Bets are built in the same way for everyone. What changes next is how the **roll is read**: each Worldview teaches the player to direct attention somewhere different.

- [**Theism**](../theism/index.md) — look for manifestation and continuity with divine action.
- [**Animism**](../animism/index.md) — look for presences that recognize one another and can form a relationship.
- [**Logic**](../logic/index.md) — measure the whole rather than isolated signs.
- [**Mysticism**](../mysticism/index.md) — look for what can be dissolved or transformed in the opposition itself.
- [**Draconic Thought**](../draconic/index.md) — look for patterns that open a choice between attachment and detachment.

## A Worldview is not a mask

A character does not choose the "best" Worldview for a scene.

A Grey Sage of Lhankor Mhy may study a Malkioni treatise, understand a sorcerous argument, and reproduce a foreign procedure without becoming a Logician. They normally remain a **Theist**: Gods, Runes, and mythical events remain operative realities of the Cosmos for them.

Likewise, an astrologer of **Buserian** or a scholar of **Irripi Ontor** may calculate, observe, classify, and reason with great rigor while inhabiting a theist world.

A Malkioni may perform a comparable calculation, but for a different ontological reason: they understand the Cosmos as intelligible through Law and Reason.

**The calculation may be the same; the world in which it makes sense is not.**

This is why Logic is not "the Worldview of intelligent people," scholars, or rational characters. The **Malkioni** — and, in another way, the **Mostali** — normally belong to this Worldview because it corresponds to their conception of the Cosmos.

## Foreign practices become Bets

Cultures circulate, trade, fight, and influence one another. They therefore borrow rites, techniques, and bodies of knowledge.

The **Pol-Joni**, for example, may adopt animist practices needed for life in Prax without ceasing to be fundamentally Orlanthi and therefore normally Theists.

The mechanical distinction is simple:

- **what the Protagonist knows, owns, practices, or has learned feeds their Bets;**
- **what they believe the world to be determines their Worldview.**

## Truly changing Worldview

A Worldview can change, but not like changing tools.

An event must truly transform the Protagonist's relationship to the Cosmos: deep conversion, an initiation that overturns their understanding of reality, an irreversible mystical experience, a draconic transformation, or another comparable shift in the fiction.

Changing Worldview does not mean deciding to look at the world differently for a moment.

**It means discovering that you can no longer look at it as you did before.**

Some forces do not replace the Worldview, however: they modify it, make it permeable, or place it in crisis. That is the role of [**Influences and transformations**](../influences/index.md).

For a more detailed discussion of cultural cases and foreign practices, see the note [A Worldview is not a mask](../../notes/lived-worldviews/index.md).
