## Inhabiting the world

The mystic does not seek to impose their will upon reality. They learn to see what, in their own will as well as in the world facing them, belongs to attachment, separation, and illusion.

Where others seek a God who answers, a spirit with whom to make a pact, or a law to understand, the mystic observes transformations.

They act, but seek not to become attached to the action. They distinguish forms without mistaking them for absolute truths. The more their practice is fulfilled, the less they need to oppose a **self** to the rest of the world.

*Others readily call them nihilists, charlatans, or madmen.*

## Reading the roll

In adversity, the mystic does not merely ask which force will prevail. They look for what, within the opposition itself, can disappear.

- Each **even** die counts as a Success.
- Each **1** may remove a **6 from the opposing roll**. The `1` and the `6` disappear together.
- Opposing `6`s are considered only after their own roll has been fully resolved, including rerolls and other effects.

This disappearance does not deny what the other side experienced. A theist genuinely received the support of their God; an animist genuinely encountered spirits. Mystical insight only reveals that, when the two realities must be compared, some manifestations can be transcended.

An apparent failure can therefore become decisive: the `1` produces no Success by itself, but it can dissolve what seemed to be the opponent's greatest strength.

## When the Worldview falters or is fulfilled

### The Veil of Duality — Weakened Worldview

The mystic can still perceive that oppositions may be transcended, but remains caught within their separation.

It now takes **two `1`s to remove a single opposing `6`**.

Unity is glimpsed, but the mystic must still struggle through duality to reach it.

### Erasure of the Self — Heroic Worldview

When the mystic truly ceases to place themselves against the world, two fulfillments become possible.

#### The Still Center

The mystic places **no personal Bet**.

Once the other side's Bets have been established, the mystic's roll simply contains **as many dice as there are Bets facing them**, with no additional justification required.

They do not search for a quality, possession, memory, or power that might justify an advantage. They no longer say: "this is my strength."

They remain at the center.

What comes to them entirely determines the shape of the trial.

#### The Empty Mirror

The mystic may instead place Bets normally.

After the rolls have been resolved, each of their `1`s may take **any die from the opposing roll**. The opposing die disappears and the `1` takes its value.

The mystic no longer opposes one form to another: for an instant, they become what faces them.

A `1` may therefore become a high even to strengthen the mystic's own result, or take a high odd whose disappearance is enough to disrupt a Logician's construction.

The mirror has no form of its own. That is precisely why it can receive every form.

## Where to look

When playing a mystic, do not look only at how many Successes you rolled.

**Look first for your `1`s. Then look at the opposing roll.**

Ask: *what seemed decisive on the other side, and can now be dissolved or transformed?*

With the **Still Center**, this attention begins before the roll: do not search for personal strengths to add to the balance. Look at what the world places before you.

## Example of play

A mystic sage sits before the entrance to a sanctuary. Four Bets stand against their intention: the guards blocking the way, their perfect discipline, the bronze gate they protect, and the sacred oath forbidding them to admit a stranger.

The sage is in **the Still Center**. Their player looks for no personal counter-Bet. Those four opposing Bets are enough to define the trial: the sage's roll also contains four dice.

The guards roll:

`2 · 4 · 6 · 6`

Four Successes.

The sage rolls:

`1 · 1 · 2 · 4`

Two Successes. But the two `1`s remove the two opposing `6`s.

Two Successes remain on each side.

Reality has not answered that the sage is stronger than the guards. It has answered that their opposition is not enough to decide the matter. The sage has added nothing to the scene. They have defeated no one. They are still sitting before the gate.

Yet something in the guards' certainty has disappeared.

> **DESIGN — Why this reading?**
>
> Mysticism is the only basic Worldview that acts directly on the opposing roll. This is not an additional attack, but a challenge to the opposition itself.
>
> The `1` is deliberately not a Success. Its power comes from its ability to make disappear what seemed decisive on the other side.
>
> **The Veil of Duality** represents a mystic who still perceives transcendence without fully inhabiting it: two signs are needed where one should suffice.
>
> The two heroic expressions explore the same idea by two paths: **Still Center**, no longer constructing a self to oppose the world; **Empty Mirror**, no longer keeping a form of one's own before what occurs. In both cases, heroic power comes from erasing the distinction between "my strength" and "what faces me."
