# Problems with Keys

Some Situations are not simply about overcoming resistance. They require understanding **what must actually be gathered or discovered before the problem can be solved**.

An investigation, puzzle, complex craft, or repair can therefore be described by several **keys**.

## Define the keys

Before or during the Situation, identify the elements required for the problem to be genuinely solved.

For example, an investigation may require discovering:

- the culprit;
- the motive;
- the method.

An alchemical preparation might require:

- a usable laboratory;
- the right ingredients;
- the correct recipe.

Keys are truths of the Situation, not boxes that must be checked in a fixed order.

## Play them

The Protagonists may obtain some keys directly through their choices and searches when nothing makes their acquisition uncertain.

When genuine uncertainty appears, use a normal resolution. The number and nature of the missing keys can then help define the opposition or a Frame Factor.

A resolution should not replace the search itself. It decides what remains uncertain after the Protagonists have chosen an approach and committed something within the Situation.

## When the problem changes

A discovery may reveal that the original keys were incomplete or misunderstood.

In that case, change the description of the problem. Glorantha Perspectives does not ask you to preserve a secret structure that has become false: the Situation changes with what the Protagonists learn and cause.
