# Situations

A **Situation** is a piece of Glorantha already in motion when the Protagonists encounter it.

Time does not prepare what the Protagonists will do. It prepares enough truths, wills, and tensions for the world to keep living when they choose where to direct their attention.

> **Do not prepare a trajectory. Prepare what is true now, then make what matters live.**

## Compact preparation

The following categories are supports, not a mandatory form. A simple Situation may fit in a few lines.

### Situation

What is true here and now before the Protagonists intervene: place, relationships, ongoing events, constraints, and important truths.

### Vector

The direction in which the Situation is already moving.

The Vector does not say what must happen. It only indicates **where the world is heading if nothing transforms it**.

### Forces

People, communities, institutions, creatures, or powers that want something and can act to obtain it.

A Force is not necessarily an adversary. Two well-intentioned Forces may become incompatible because they do not see the same thing or protect the same truths.

### Pressures

What pushes the Situation to change: a deadline, an approaching rite, an army on the move, a debt, growing fear, a dwindling resource.

Pressures remind the table that **the world continues without the Protagonists**.

### Promises

A **Promise** is a remarkable possibility that belongs to the world: an image, revelation, mythical manifestation, human moment, or Gloranthan detail that Time would like to make live if the fiction naturally opens that space.

A Promise is not a scene to force. It prescribes no trajectory for the scenario.

Promises also give Time a way to **show the wonder of Glorantha** without waiting for a Protagonist to ask for it. The world can surprise, reveal, and manifest its myths and strangeness on its own initiative. In that sense, **Glorantha almost becomes a Protagonist**: not because it imposes a story, but because it acts, responds, and reveals itself.

A useful test is simple:

> **Would this moment remain remarkable if another Protagonist were here?**

If so, the Promise probably belongs to the world rather than to an arc prepared for the character.

## What about Bets?

The Situation does not pre-write resolutions. It may point to truths that could matter, but **Bets**, Frame Factors, Intentions, and Focuses only emerge in contact with what the players actually decide to do.

The same element may therefore help, hinder, close a possibility, or carry no weight at all depending on the Focus.

## Complete example

[**The Three Stones Spring**](three-stones-source.md) shows a Situation prepared from only a few elements: four Forces, three Pressures, a world that will change at dawn, and a Promise that belongs to no Protagonist.

This Situation was then actually played with Peek-ee-Peek; the [Verbatim and Time's Backstage](../../narratives/adventures-in-prax/three-stones-source/) let you compare directly **what was prepared** with **what play made of it**.
