# Example — The Three Stones Spring

This example shows a Situation small enough to prepare in a few minutes, yet dense enough to generate play without prescribing scenes or solutions.

Peek-ee-Peek, a Sable Rider and daughter of Khan Paak the Cunning, reaches a spring in the Wastes. A Lunar work party escorted by Sables is preparing a water station for the road to **Moonbroth**. Work begins at dawn and, according to the surveyor, requires moving one of the three stones that guard the spring.

The animals already refuse to drink. The local spirit, **Mother-of-Reeds**, has fallen silent.

## Vector

At dawn, the project moves from surveying to construction. If nothing transforms the Situation, the great stone will be moved and the spring will change.

The Vector does not tell Peek what to do. It simply describes **the world's current movement**.

## Forces

- **Ostar Varenis**, surveyor from the Moonbroth garrison. He wants a water station for the Imperial road. He is not malicious: he does not perceive the spirit and sees an inconvenient stone where Peek sees a presence.
- **Hirrak and the Sable escort.** They want to honor Paak's word and bring home the promised money. Hirrak loves his sister, but he gave his word before the Khan.
- **Mother-of-Reeds.** She tolerates drinking, but not touching the stones. If the stone is moved, she will leave or take revenge.
- **The other tribes.** The spring is shared under Praxian custom. A guarded Lunar cistern would change who may drink there.

None of these Forces is designated as the "enemy." Their incompatibility is enough to place the world under tension.

## Pressures

- Work begins at dawn.
- It is the full moon, and Peek's sacrifice to the ancestors cannot wait.
- The animals already refuse to drink.

**If Peek does nothing**, the porters move the stone in the morning. The spring turns foul for days, and a neighboring tribe accuses the Sables of defiling a spirit place.

## What might matter

Preparation may remind Time of a few truths without turning them into Bets in advance:

- Peek's taboo — "never defile a place inhabited by a spirit" — could become a **Frame Factor**;
- *Daughter of the Khan*, *Bargain with spirits*, *Customs of Prax*, *Sense of superiority*, *Strong opinions*, or *Impulsive* might matter depending on her Intention;
- Paak's word to the Empire, the armed escort, and Hirrak himself may weigh the other way;
- Peek reads reality through **Animism**, and if her relationship to the Moon becomes relevant that night, the **Full Moon Lunar Influence** may pass through her Worldview.

None of this is a balance yet. The Focus will decide what truly matters, and for which outcome.

## Promise

Under the full moon, tradition says Mother-of-Reeds sometimes shows **two moons in the water: the red and a white one**. No one knows what the second means.

This Promise belongs to the world, not to Peek. It would remain remarkable if another Protagonist stood at this spring that night.

Time therefore does not need to lead Peek toward the image. It only needs to know the image exists **if the fiction gives it room to appear**.

## See what the Situation became

This preparation was actually played. [**Adventures in Prax — The Three Stones Spring**](../../narratives/adventures-in-prax/three-stones-source/index.md) publishes the play Verbatim and Time's Backstage without reconstructing, after the fact, a trajectory that did not exist before play.
