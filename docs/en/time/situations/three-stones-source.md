# The Three Stones Source

This example uses the preparation for a Situation actually played with **Peek-ee-Peek**. It shows how little material is needed when the prepared elements can already act upon one another.

## Situation

At the **Three Stones Source**, in the Wastes, a Lunar surveyor is preparing a water relay for the imperial road. Work is due to begin at dawn. To dig the cistern, he intends to move the largest of the three standing stones guarding the spring.

The animals already refuse to drink. **Mother-of-Reeds**, the spirit of the spring, has fallen silent and withdrawn against the stone.

It is the night of the **Full Moon**.

## Forces

- **Ostar Varenis**, surveyor from the garrison of **Moonbroth**. He wants a water relay for the imperial road. He is not malicious: he does not perceive the spirit and sees an inconvenient stone where others see a presence.
- **Hirrak and the Sable escort.** They want to honour Paak's word and bring back the promised money. Hirrak loves his sister, but he gave his word before the Khan.
- **Mother-of-Reeds.** She tolerates drinking, but not touching the stones. If the stone is moved, she will leave or take revenge.
- **The other tribes.** The spring is shared under Praxian custom. A guarded Lunar cistern would change who may drink there.

## Pressures

- Work begins at dawn.
- It is the full moon, and the sacrifice to the ancestors cannot wait.
- The animals already refuse to drink.

## If no one acts

The porters move the stone in the morning. The spring turns foul for days, and a neighbouring tribe accuses the Sables of defiling a spirit-place.

## What might matter

The preparation imposes no Focus. It only points to truths that might become relevant depending on what the Protagonists do:

- a taboo against defiling a place inhabited by a spirit may close off some possibilities and become a **Frame Factor**;
- the authority of a **Khan's daughter**, the **customs of Prax**, an ability to **bargain with spirits**, pride, or impulsiveness may become Bets in very different Foci;
- on the other side may weigh **Paak's word given to the Empire**, the armed escort, Ostar's knowledge, or Hirrak himself;
- if a Protagonist's relationship to the Moon truly matters that night, the **Full Moon Lunar Influence** may pass through their Worldview.

These are not a prepared list of bonuses. Most of them may never enter a resolution.

## Promise: the two moons

Under the full moon, tradition says that Mother-of-Reeds sometimes shows **two moons in the water: the red one and a white one**. No one knows what the second means.

This Promise belongs to the world, not to Peek-ee-Peek. It would remain true with another Protagonist present, and it may never be seen if the fiction does not naturally open that moment.

## What the example prepares — and what it does not

The Situation does not say:

- how the works will be stopped or allowed;
- whether the Lunars are right or wrong;
- whether Hirrak will support his sister;
- whether Mother-of-Reeds will remain;
- whether the Promise of the two moons will be revealed;
- which resolution will happen.

It prepares only **a local world under tension**.
