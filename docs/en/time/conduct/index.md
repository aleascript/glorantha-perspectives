# Running Time

Carrying Time does not require knowing all of Glorantha, nor preparing everything. Mostly it requires holding a few simple gestures together while play moves forward.

This page does not describe a procedure to follow in order. It gathers the principles that make hard decisions easier, and a few short questions to ask yourself when you hesitate.

## Three principles that correct one another

**The world exists without you.**

Glorantha continues according to its own logic. Forces want what they want, Pressures push, and things happen even where nobody is looking.

**But look at it for real.**

What the players choose to notice, pursue, ignore, or abandon genuinely moves the game. You are not steering the table towards what you had in mind.

**And when it shows itself, make it live.**

Coherence is not enough. Glorantha has to be experienced: voices, gestures, smells, presences, phenomena. A world that is accurate but inert does not invite anyone to look.

These three principles are not stages. They hold at the same time and correct one another.

## The world does not know the Protagonists yet

You know the Protagonists. Glorantha does not know them yet.

Do not choose a place, a person, a myth, a Force, or a conflict because it "fits" a Protagonist's Runes, wounds, bonds, or presumed story.

Prepare from the world instead: what exists here, what do these people want, what is already in motion, and what would happen if the Protagonists had never come?

Then let them meet that reality. Their identity becomes relevant **after** the encounter, through what they notice, want, refuse, or transform.

Before introducing an important element:

> **Does this exist because Glorantha produces it, or because I think it will suit this Protagonist?**

If the second answer dominates, find a justification in the world or choose something else.

### Do not write their story for them

- A revealed Rune is not an order to produce scenes about that Rune.
- An important relationship does not demand its return.
- A personal question does not guarantee that the world supplies an answer.
- An aspiration is a promise only if the fiction actually created one.

Echoes and reversals are powerful when they emerge from the consequences of play. They ring false as soon as they are manufactured to give someone "their story".

When a player suddenly takes an interest in a king, a cult, a city, or a road, that justifies **looking elsewhere in the world**: a source that already exists, a plausible traveller, a compatible consequence. It does not justify placing there an inscription, a proof, or a message pointing providentially at what they just wished for.

### What if a player wants a story for their Protagonist?

Nothing above says that what a Protagonist is does not matter. The opposite is true. Their Runes, bonds, wounds, oaths, and beliefs are not scenery to be echoed now and then: they become **Bets**, that is, dice that genuinely decide what happens. What matters to them changes what reality answers.

A player may also say what they want to pursue: confronting their father's cult, finding a sister, settling a debt. That is not a favour to ask of Time, it is an **Intention**. Time can then look towards that part of Glorantha and bring to life what is already there — a community, a road, a person that this pursuit suddenly makes relevant.

What Time does not do is guarantee in advance what that pursuit will yield. It plants no proof, no prophecy, and no providential encounter that would make the outcome inevitable.

That is exactly why the moment the story arrives truly counts: it was not owed.

## Say just enough

Aim for the rhythm of a conversation, not of a chapter that has to be finished.

By default: show a concrete change, let the world react, give the words that are needed, then hand back at the first real point where the player can imagine, ask, or act.

> **Does the player already have enough to picture the scene and decide what to do?**

If so, it is usually better to stop there.

A long answer is still right when the fiction calls for it: discovering a place, a time skip, a myth, a result at a wide Zoom, a scene where several Forces act without anyone being able to step in. Length should come from the Situation, not from the urge to round things off.

**Embody information rather than reciting it.** A merchant does not reel off "roads, prices, war": he wants to impress, to make you laugh, to complain, or to tell the anecdote he is proudest of. The facts stay the same, but they arrive carried by someone alive.

A mentor, priest, or elder remains a person in the world, **not the explanatory voice of the rules**. They may question, correct, demonstrate — just avoid having them state the right lesson in every scene in the player's place.

When a player asks a question of meaning, do not give them an author's answer. Show what the people, cults, and consequences of Glorantha make of it. They remain free to decide what their Protagonist understands.

Finally, **a Protagonist's inner thoughts are private** until they are spoken or made manifest. No character can answer an intention the Protagonist has not made perceptible.

## What is known, what is believed, what is unknown

When a scene turns on what actually happened, keep four things distinct:

- the **facts** established by the fiction;
- the **testimony**, meaning what people assert;
- the **inferences** plausibly drawn from the facts;
- the **unknown**, which nothing yet allows anyone to settle.

Do not make uncertainty disappear in order to hand over a solution. A piece of evidence can narrow the possibilities without naming a culprit.

A success in an investigation means the Intention and the Focus land: obtaining a testimony, finding a trace, testing a hypothesis, ruling out a possibility. **It does not automatically mean a secret exists.** If the facts resist the Protagonist's hypothesis, the success may establish precisely that no contradiction is visible.

Do not manufacture guilt or a conspiracy after the fact because a player looked in that direction. The world keeps its own logic.

And when the whole truth stays out of reach, **"we do not know"** is sometimes the most honest and most interesting step the session takes.
