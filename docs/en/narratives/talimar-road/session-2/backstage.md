# Time Backstage

:::warning[Spoilers]

This page reveals the preparation and improvisations of Session 2. Threads that remain secret for future sessions are not published here.

:::

## The prepared Situation

Session 2 resumed directly from a promise left by the fiction of Session 1: if **Karest** survived and could speak, the healer from the House of Peace would send word to the Sages.

The preparation was deliberately narrow:

- Karest had made it through the night;
- he had awakened and could speak, but only briefly;
- his account would remain **testimony**, not automatic truth;
- Beren's kin were preparing the funeral rites;
- the question of compensation and law could continue even without absolute certainty.

The essential point was not to turn Karest's awakening into a “solution” to the investigation. Time had to preserve the distinction between **established facts, testimony, inference, and the unknown**.

No new thread connected to Tal's Portrait had been prepared for him. The Situation simply had to continue according to its own logic.

## If Tal had done nothing unexpected

The most likely course was simple: the healer came to fetch Hendrik and Tal, Karest gave his version of events within the limits of his condition, and the Sages could then hand over to both groups what the investigation now allowed them to say.

Even after Karest's testimony, there was no necessity for the affair to produce complete truth. The clans could perfectly well have to decide on reparation from a case that remained incomplete.

## What Tal made emerge: the night outing

The first major divergence was proposed entirely by the player.

Before resuming the morning scene, Tal declared that he had been unable to sleep and had gone prowling around the House of Peace. He knew this conduct might be questionable for a Grey Sage, but the affair unsettled him enough that he took the risk of searching for a detail he might have missed.

Before leaving, he invented a small ritual addressed to Lhankor Mhy:

> **“The eyes see. The ears hear. The head understands. Together they serve the search for Truth.”**

Time did not turn it into a new power. The ritual simply became a **Bet**: a fictional way for Tal to discipline his attention and place his search under the gaze of his God.

The resolution covered an entire Sequence: prowl around the House discreetly enough to overhear something genuinely useful to the investigation without being discovered as an observer.

Tal committed:

- Truth put into action through his ritual;
- his precise knowledge of the affair;
- neutral clothing.

The world opposed him with:

- the House of Peace's normal night-time vigilance;
- the extra attention surrounding a wounded man at the heart of a dispute;
- Tal's unfamiliarity with the place's night-time routines.

The first state led to a **Status Quo**: Tal found a safe observation point but learned nothing genuinely useful and was not discovered.

The player then chose to raise the stakes: Tal deliberately left the safety of his vantage point and moved closer to the walls and openings.

A procedural correction was needed during play: raising the stakes after a Status Quo adds one Bet **to both sides**. The correct resolution was therefore **4 against 4**. Tal scored 4 Successes; Time, read through the Middle World, scored 3. **Victory for Tal, 4–3.**

## “He still had the knife”

The conversation between the two healers was not prepared.

It was improvised as the manifestation of the night-time Victory: Tal had to obtain **something genuinely useful**, without receiving proof that would close the affair.

The choice was therefore a spontaneous statement by Karest, spoken in an uncertain state of consciousness before any questioning:

> **“He still had the knife.”**

The phrase had several useful properties for the Situation:

- it was new to Tal;
- it had been heard directly by a healer who could be certain of the words;
- it came before the formal interview and therefore could not have been shaped to fit the investigation;
- yet it remained **a statement made by Karest**, not proof that Beren was still attacking.

When Karest repeated exactly the same phrase the next day, the consistency of his account increased, but the fiction did not turn that consistency into certainty.

The Debrief revealed a tool that could have helped Time choose this manifestation: **Runic Inspiration**. Once the Victory had been established, the result already said what had to be true; a Rune could have inspired the form taken by that truth without changing it. A Rune such as **Truth** would naturally have pointed toward precise words or testimony; a second Rune would only have been useful if the first had not been enough to make a concrete manifestation appear.

## Karest's interview

Karest had not been prepared as a secret culprit or a hidden innocent.

The player began with an open question: recount the raid, his wound, and what had happened before and after. Karest then gave a version broadly compatible with Enissa's: Beren had wounded him, had fallen or slipped, still held his knife, had struck upward, and Karest had answered once with his spear.

Tal's second question was much more accusatory: had Karest moved toward Beren in order to take him into death with him?

The answer was improvised from the Situation: Karest acknowledged his anger and that he believed he had nearly been killed, but denied walking toward Beren intending to die with him. His phrase — **“If he'd dropped his knife… I don't know what I would have done after that”** — preserved a messier human truth than a clean exoneration.

This statement should not be read as objective truth about his inner state. **Only Karest can testify to his own intent**, and his testimony remains testimony.

The healer ended the interview before Tal could return to Darin's “Enough.” That point therefore remained unresolved.

## From investigation to report

The conversation with Hendrik tightened the formulation:

- “Karest seems sincere” is Tal's judgment;
- Enissa and Karest give two matching testimonies about Beren's knife and movement;
- no direct witness says they saw Beren unarmed at that moment;
- the body is not incompatible with those accounts;
- Darin still claims to have heard Beren say “Enough”;
- that claim is not enough to establish that Beren had actually stopped fighting.

Time then improvised, from the Situation's social logic, that the representatives might seek to meet before the end of the day. Hendrik's decision to ask both sides to hear **the same report together**, then to let Tal speak, had not been prepared either.

## Resolving the report

The heart of the final resolution was not: “Does Tal know the facts?”

He did.

The uncertainty was social: **could he make his report recognized as sufficiently faithful, precise, and impartial to become a shared basis for judgment?**

The first exchange resulted in a **Status Quo, 2–2**. The point of friction was tiny but essential for a Sage: Tal had transformed Darin's precise claim — “I recognized Beren saying Enough” — into an uncertainty that almost concerned who had spoken the words at all.

The Status Quo therefore meant neither rejection nor acceptance of the report. The assembly simply asked him to distinguish what Darin claimed from what the investigation allowed them to establish.

Tal raised the stakes. He acknowledged Darin's testimony exactly, then explained why he had **minimized** it: about twenty paces away, amid the noise of a fight.

This time, Tal scored 4 Successes against 5: **Defeat without domination**.

The consequence was not that the report collapsed. The assembly accepted the substance of the facts, but refused to let the Sage himself make the passage from **reporting testimony** to **deciding its value for judgment**. The report had to be corrected on that specific point.

The man with the tablet addressing him as **“young Sage”** was experienced by Tal as a judgment on his youth. The Debrief confirmed that this reading belongs to the Protagonist: the in-world character's form of address was legitimate, and Tal's feeling of unfairness is precisely material for play.

## What the clans did with uncertainty

Once the report had been handed over, Time played out the discussion among the representatives.

The important point was not to finally reveal “what had really happened,” but to show people forced to act without possessing that complete truth.

A form of agreement emerged:

- the Greenhaft would acknowledge a debt for the death that occurred during their raid and offer compensation;
- the Blueberry would not demand that Karest be declared a murderer on the basis of a surrender that could not be established.

The fiction thus showed that uncertainty does not necessarily prevent social action. It can instead force people to decide **how far they can go without pretending to know more**.

## Improvised lore: plausibility before encyclopedia

During play, a question arose about Beren's and Karest's clans and about the likely form of such a meeting in Jonstown.

The names **Blueberry** and **Greenhaft**, and their membership in the Cinsina tribe, were used as plausible extrapolations from the lore available at the time of the scene. The Debrief clarified the principle for running the game: Time should know Glorantha well enough to **reason from the lore**. When an exact detail is unknown but minor, Time can choose the most plausible option and keep playing. If the detail changes an important decision, the uncertainty should be acknowledged and verification can happen outside the session.

Play should become neither improvisation without grounding nor an encyclopedia search that stops at every detail.

## Another possibility: temporarily delegating Time

The Debrief revealed a possibility that was not used during this session.

After Tal's report, the Player could temporarily have carried one of the two clans — for example, the Greenhaft — while Time carried the Blueberry. Tal would have remained silent in the fiction, but the table would have played the negotiation directly instead of watching it entirely as a scene between non-player characters.

This idea follows from GP's own vocabulary: **Time is a game function, not necessarily a fixed person**. Some of its responsibilities can therefore be redistributed locally, provided it is clear who carries what, all established facts are respected, and the previous distribution is then clearly restored.

## What the playtest showed

This second part of the affair confirms several properties already visible in Session 1.

First, an investigation can continue to produce play without producing a total solution. Karest's night-time phrase, his morning interview, and the matching testimonies reduce uncertainty without abolishing it.

Second, **precision itself can become conflictual**. Tal is not mainly mistaken about the facts; he crosses the boundary between saying exactly what a witness claims and weighing the value of that testimony himself while transmitting it.

Finally, the Situation continues after the Sage's work is done. Tal hands an incomplete truth to people who must live with it, negotiate, repair, or quarrel. The session thus discovers another aspect of Lhankor Mhy's role: not only seeking and preserving knowledge, but accepting that once it has been formulated precisely enough, **it no longer belongs entirely to him**.
