# Time Backstage

:::warning[Spoilers]

This page reveals the preparation and improvisations of Session 1. Threads that remain secret for future sessions are not published here.

:::

## The prepared Situation

The Jonstown Situation had not been written as a “test of Law and Truth” for Talimar.

It was meant to exist **independently of him**, arising from the ordinary functioning of two Gloranthan institutions: the **House of Peace** protects those its healers are treating; the **Jonstown Library** can provide Grey Sages to gather testimony, research precedents, and shed light on a matter.

The starting point was deliberately ordinary:

- **Karest**, badly wounded, is under the protection of a healer of Chalana Arroy;
- **Beren** is dead after a cattle raid;
- the dead man's kin accuse Karest of crossing the line between combat and homicide;
- Karest's kin maintain that Beren died during the raid and refuse to let him be treated as a murderer before the facts have been examined;
- as long as Karest remains under Chalana Arroy's care, dragging him out of the temple or striking him would be a grave transgression.

There was **no secret murder, no conspiracy, and no hidden solution** for Tal to discover.

## Forces

### The House of Peace

It heals without distinction and protects whoever is under its care. This protection prevents the quarrel from immediately resolving through violence and shifts the conflict toward what can later be said, proved, and judged.

### Beren's kin

They want his death recognized as a wrong if it was one, so that reparation may follow. Not all of them are equally patient with procedure.

### Karest's kin

They want to prevent a wounded man who still cannot speak from already being turned into a murderer by the opposing account.

### Jonstown

The city has an interest in preventing a clan quarrel from degenerating in its streets.

### The Library and the Sages

They can record, investigate, compare, and advise. They are not there to manufacture a convenient truth that will calm everyone down.

## Pressures

- Karest will not remain under Chalana Arroy's care forever.
- Contradictory versions of the death are already circulating.
- The more kin and companions arrive, the harder peace becomes to maintain.
- If the situation degenerates, the authorities of Jonstown may intervene much more brutally.

## If Tal had not been there

The Situation did not depend on the Protagonist.

Hendrik and other adults would have gathered testimony, sought the facts, and brought the matter before the relevant authorities or people. The quarrel might have been contained through procedure and compensation, or hardened depending on the actions of the two families.

**Tal was neither the cause of the problem nor its necessary solution.**

That was an important condition of the preparation: the world had to continue existing without reorganizing itself around him.

## What was prepared

Time had therefore prepared:

- the House of Peace and its protection;
- the two groups of kin;
- the accusation arising from a cattle raid;
- several incompatible testimonies;
- the social and temporal pressure;
- the function of the Library and the Sages as investigators and advisers;
- the entry into play: a healer comes looking for a Grey Sage, Hendrik takes Tal with him, then refuses to tell him immediately what to look at.

The details of the affair had not been fixed beyond what was necessary for the Situation to hold together.

## What play made emerge

The first surprise was how immediately Tal mobilized **Law** and **Truth** as almost absolute requirements. Yet the player begins by lucidly identifying a posture that might distract him from what matters: **not shaming Hendrik** and **being worthy of Lhankor Mhy** are not themselves Law and Truth. But faced with the two groups, Tal almost immediately turns his new Runes into authority: since vengeance is not justice, he orders the kin to leave so that Truth can be sought more cleanly.

The player himself notices that his speech is more peremptory than he intended and that it may overshadow Hendrik. The tension between **the posture of the young Sage** and **the actual function of the Sage** is therefore present before the first roll even happens.

Another discovery developed throughout the Situation without ever being reduced to a single maxim. Until now, Tal had encountered his vocation mainly through **myths, texts, questions, the search for knowledge, and the possibility of travel**. Jonstown shows him its social side: a healer comes looking for a Sage; families expect him to examine facts; witnesses speak to him; two groups must then decide how to act based on what the Sages can honestly state.

The role of Grey Sage therefore ceases to be only that of someone who **seeks and preserves knowledge**. Tal also discovers someone whose knowledge and method have a function in the everyday life of the community, precisely when accounts, interests, and emotions make it hard to know what people can act upon.

His hypothesis that Karest might have killed Beren out of an old rage also arose from play, from the previous history between the two men.

More importantly, Tal encountered **a human corpse at close range for the first time**. This concrete encounter with Death was not a prepared scene. It followed directly from his choice to examine Beren's body.

Finally, the session produced an equally important epistemological shift without that having been planned: Tal moved from an almost absolute Truth toward a **responsibility of formulation**.

The question gradually ceased to be:

> “What is the whole truth?”

and became:

> **“What are we entitled to say that we know?”**

The lasting consequence chosen by the player was:

> **“Seek and report only established facts.”**

Hendrik added a complementary requirement: when the facts do not allow a conclusion, a Sage must know how to say **“we do not know.”**

These two transformations are not the same. The second clarifies **how Tal must seek and speak about Truth**; the first transforms **what it means to him to be a Grey Sage among other people**.

## What was improvised

Much of the concrete detail was created in response to the player's choices:

- the precise wording of Darin's and Enissa's testimonies;
- the personal history between Beren and Karest;
- Beren's provocations after their previous conflict;
- the physical details observed on the body;
- Enissa's gestural reconstruction;
- several of Hendrik's teaching formulations.

The examination of the body is particularly revealing. It had to provide information because Tal had chosen that path, but not become an oracle. Time therefore improvised a configuration compatible with several readings: **a single mortal wound, with Beren lower than Karest**, and no way for the body alone to say whether he was begging for mercy or still fighting.

Likewise, success against Enissa did not produce the contradiction Tal hoped for. The reconstruction became more precise — and **remained compatible with the material evidence**.

## Two resolutions, two very different consequences

The first resolution concerned the young Grey Sage's social authority before the two groups. Tal invoked **Law**, **Truth**, and the fact that Karest could not flee to ask everyone else to leave; he himself identified the emotional tension, his young age, and the possibility of irritating Hendrik as risks. He scored **3 Successes against 4**.

The Defeat did not mean “the investigation fails.” It meant exactly what the Focus had put at stake: Tal did not yet have the authority needed to impose his order on everyone. Nobody left. On the other hand, the conflict his intervention stirred made Darin emerge as a witness.

The second resolution concerned Enissa. Tal wanted to obtain a reconstruction precise enough to compare her account with the material facts. He scored **1 Success against 0**.

The Victory therefore did not mean “Tal discovers that Enissa is lying.” It produced what the Focus asked for: **more precise and testable testimony**. And that testimony survived the comparison.

The session thus shows something very characteristic of GP: **succeeding at an investigation does not mean the game manufactures a solution**. A success can make uncertainty more precise.

## Time's mistakes

Two clear mistakes were identified during play.

First, Time made Hendrik react to an internal thought of Tal's that had never been spoken. The player pointed it out immediately, and play resumed with that thought correctly treated as private.

Then, a line from Beren's mother accidentally inverted the logic of the testimonies around “Enough” and had to be explicitly corrected.

The first mistake provides a simple vigilance rule: **thoughts described by the player remain private unless they are spoken or manifested in the fiction.**

Another risk appeared with Hendrik himself. He can easily become the teacher who always states the “right lesson.” If he does so too often, Tal learns by listening to his mentor rather than by colliding with the world. The end of the session works better: Tal himself formulates what he thinks can be said to the clans; Hendrik only tightens the wording.

## What the playtest showed about GP

The affair had been designed as a small local quarrel. Without planning it, play turned it into both a **practical initiation into the epistemology of Lhankor Mhy** and a **discovery of the Grey Sage's social role**.

The almost mundane nature of the Situation mattered. Glorantha did not offer the young initiate a cosmic mystery calibrated to his new vocation. It gave him a dead man, kin, witnesses, a House of Peace, laws, and two clans that would have to keep living after the investigation.

It is precisely within that ordinariness that Tal discovers that knowledge is not confined to the Library. A Sage may be called because a community must act while nobody possesses the whole truth. Gathering, comparing, formulating, and knowing how to say “we do not know” then become social acts as much as intellectual ones.

The autonomy of everyday life strengthened the sense that the world was not handing out scenes designed to “advance Tal's arc.” An ordinary Situation could nevertheless become deeply significant **because Tal encountered it as the person he had become**.

The session therefore leaves two lasting lessons, connected but irreducible to one another: **do not attribute to the world more certainty than it gives us**, and discover that this discipline has a concrete use for the people who will have to live, decide, and reconcile — or not — on the basis of what the Sage has said.
