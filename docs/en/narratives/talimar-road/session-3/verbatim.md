But the texts make an interesting distinction. Among the Heortlings, its appearance had been received as an ill omen, and old prophecies already named it as an enemy. Yet the first red-clad missionaries come to Sartar **only after Sartar's apotheosis**. It is then, the chronicles say, that the people of the kingdom truly begin to learn who the Red Goddess is and what the Lunars claim she is.

During his reign, however, Sartar exchanges ambassadors with the king of Tarsh to encourage peace and trade.

So you find a fairly clear answer to your question: **Sartar lived his entire life under the Red Moon, but the Sartar he founded did not yet know the Lunars as later generations would know them.**

What you do not find, however, is a clear statement by Sartar himself saying what he thought of this new power to the north. The biographies recount his alliances, foundations, oaths, roads, and political marriages far better than his opinion of the Moon.

If you want to continue, this is where the research becomes harder: no longer asking *when* the Moon was there, but **what Sartar himself knew and thought about it**.

He hands the document back to you. This time, the affair is truly closed.

When you then speak to him of Sartar, the roads, Tarsh, and finally Boldhome, Hendrik takes longer before answering.

— What do I think of Sartar?

He looks at the road ahead of you.

— That he achieved something the Heortlings rarely manage for long: getting people with excellent reasons to quarrel to work together.

A small smile.

— Roads, towns, markets, shared temples... It sounds less heroic than fighting a god. Until the day you understand that all of it created a kingdom.

Then, to your question about Boldhome:

— **One day, certainly. Not today.**

He settles his pack back onto his shoulder.

— And if you keep asking questions about Sartar at this rate, I imagine you'll find your own reason to go before anyone sends you there.

Behind you, Jonstown's gates recede.

Ahead of you, the road resumes.
