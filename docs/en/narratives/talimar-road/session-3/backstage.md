# Time Backstage

:::warning[Spoilers]

This page reveals the preparation, improvisations, consolidation retcons, and Debrief of Session 3. Secrets that remain active for future sessions are not published here.

:::

## The prepared Situation: the Sage's legacy

Session 3 prepared no trajectory for Tal.

Time began from an ordinary logic of the Jonstown Library: it receives gifts, legacies, and documents entrusted to Sages; some are misfiled, some remain tied to families or clans, and the boundary between **what was in a Sage's possession** and **what belonged to him** can become contentious.

The prepared Situation was therefore this: **Jorstan, son of Argrathor**, a Grey Sage of Jonstown, had just died after a long illness. His written effects and several chests were arriving at the Library as what his fellow Sages considered his legacy to the temple.

His kin, however, disputed that a small bundle of tablets and parchments formed part of the legacy. According to them, these documents were family archives entrusted to Jorstan for copying and safekeeping, not property that belonged to him.

### Vector

The unloading was taking place at the entrance to the Library. Until an agreement was reached, the bundle physically remained in the family's hands while the servants and Sages treated it as part of the legacy.

### Forces

- **The temple and the Grey Sages** wanted to preserve the integrity of the legacy and avoid a precedent in which a family could arbitrarily reclaim documents deposited with the temple simply by asserting ownership.
- **Jorstan's kin** wanted to recover what they regarded as ancestral archives that had never ceased to belong to them.
- **The servants and apprentices** wanted to finish the physical work, avoid a scandal, and let the Library continue functioning.
- **Time** pressed the scene forward: the longer the dispute remained public on the steps, the more humiliating it became for both sides.

### Pressures

- No one wanted open violence in the Library, but either side could try to create a fait accompli by keeping or carrying away the documents.
- The bundle must not be damaged.
- Hendrik had no special authority over the legacy: he was simply a Sage who happened to be present.

### Promises left open

The bundle's exact contents were not prepared beyond their plausible nature as family or clan archives. They were meant to become concrete only if Tal opened it.

Jorstan might have left traces that could clarify his intention, but no decisive proof was guaranteed. The conflict could be resolved legally, ritually, socially, materially — or remain unresolved.

The discipline of preparation was explicit: **do not present this Situation as a test meant for Tal**. If he ignored it, it would simply continue without him.

## What Tal did with the Situation

Tal first chose to intervene.

He proposed taking personal charge of the bundle in order to examine it, presenting his intervention as a way out of the immediate confrontation. That proposal opened the session's only formal resolution.

Once the bundle was opened, the fiction specified its contents from what the Situation required: lists of births, marriages, and deaths; land agreements; finally, an old tablet to which the father of Jorstan and Enastina had attached particular importance.

**Enastina**, Jorstan's sister, and **Argrathor**, their father, had not been prepared beyond the kinship required by the Situation. Their characters and details emerged through Tal's questions.

Argrathor became a man who raised cattle, sat in assemblies, and possessed an exceptional memory for oaths, lands, and relations between families. Jorstan's phrase — **“a library in his head and no patience for books”** — was improvised to make that memory tangible without turning Argrathor into a Sage.

## A staging ambiguity: what Enastina was claiming

The preparation said that the family disputed **the entire bundle**.

During the scene, however, Time gave much greater weight to the thick tablet: Argrathor had explicitly said it must never leave the family. The player therefore quite reasonably understood that this tablet was the heart of the claim and that the rest might be surrendered to the temple more easily.

When Enastina then continued to claim the other documents, she appeared to have changed her position.

An `Off:` exchange clarified the distinction:

- the entire bundle was claimed as family property entrusted to Jorstan;
- the thick tablet had **in addition** a specific injunction from Argrathor.

The problem was therefore not Enastina's inconsistency, but a lack of clarity in Time's staging.

Lesson: when a conflict depends on positions of ownership, testimony, or law, one clear sentence establishing **exactly what each side claims** can prevent an ambiguity of narration from artificially becoming an ambiguity of character.

## The session's real movement: Tal leaves

The decisive moment of the session was not prepared.

Tal eventually abandoned the dispute to Hendrik. He left the Library and rediscovered an old dissatisfaction from his time with his uncle **Grimar**: he had loved the journeys but not the merchandise; here, he loved knowledge but not the feeling that he was becoming a notary for local disputes.

That movement belongs entirely to the Player.

The small caravan glimpsed from the hill was originally only an element of urban scenery. Tal chose to go to it. It then became an opening onto the wider world: **Daran, son of Orlev**, Alda-Chur, Tarsh, Furthest, inspections on the roads, rumors of soldiers, and contradictory news.

Time had not prepared Daran as an important NPC. He became important because Tal chose to look in that direction.

## Jorstan and Argrathor: the affair closes anyway

When Tal later returned to search the archives discreetly, Time had to answer a factual question: had Jorstan left any trace of the documents' status?

The preparation allowed for that possibility without guaranteeing it.

The answer chosen was restrained and not magically decisive: a working register carried the notes **“Argrathor's papers — from the household. To copy.”** and then **“originals to be returned after copying.”** The thick tablet appeared as **“old partition — father's original.”**

These traces were enough to give a strong factual basis to the family's claim. Tal nevertheless did not use them to re-enter the scene. He assumed Hendrik would find the same thing and went to dinner.

That choice mattered: the Situation could resolve without forcing Tal to become its center.

## The mismatch in breath

The Debrief revealed that the prepared Situation did indeed respect the principle **“the world does not know the Protagonist”**, but matched the Player's appetite poorly that day.

The player likes Glorantha's realism, but this time wanted more **epic scope, adventure, wonder, and horizon**. The archival dispute therefore felt narrow and irritating.

That irritation did not break play. It became game material: Tal withdrew, sought the road and news of the wider world, then chose for himself to study **Sartar**.

The session therefore highlights a strong property of GP: the Player does not decree what exists in the world, but exerts very powerful control over **where they direct their gaze**. That gaze can move the campaign without any trajectory having been prepared for them.

## Toward a “Session Breath”?

A design possibility emerged in the Debrief: distinguishing two levels that are not contradictory.

- **Glorantha does not know the Protagonist.** The world must not manufacture tailor-made events to fulfill their arc.
- **Time knows the Players.** At a table, Time can know that today they want roads, wonder, dark tension, lightness, or larger stakes.

This perhaps suggests a very light table-contract tool: a **Session Breath** or **Session Horizon**, expressed freely in a sentence rather than through a mandatory grid.

For example:

> “Today I'm more in the mood for roads, wonder, and larger stakes; less local politics.”

That signal would be neither a Bet, nor a Promise, nor an order addressed to the world. It could simply help Time choose **where to open the camera** when a new Situation needs to appear.

The point would precisely be not to increase Time's workload: this is not about preparing several scenarios to satisfy every possible appetite, but about allowing a Situation that does not catch on to continue off-screen, then opening another plausible piece of the world.

## Glorantha must also take the stage

The principle **“the world does not know the Protagonist”** protects the autonomy of the fiction very well. But this session reveals a symmetrical danger: interpreted too timidly, it can lead Time merely to simulate everyday life honestly and produce a coherent but flat world.

Yet the point is not to simulate just any everyday life. The point is to make **Glorantha** live. In dramaturgical terms, the world itself is one of the essential protagonists of play: its Runes, myths, gods, spirits, peoples, rites, landscapes, scales, and impossibilities must sometimes impose their presence.

This is where **Promises** and the **Wow effect** become important. Time can carry a few latent images or possibilities — spectacular, ontological, retrospective, or simply human and unforgettable — without building a trajectory toward them. They are not meant for Tal: they belong to the world. A useful test is to ask: **would this moment still be remarkable if another Protagonist were here instead?** If so, the Wow probably comes from Glorantha rather than from targeting the character.

Wonder does not have to be constantly cosmic. A Rune that truly manifests, a strange custom experienced rather than explained, a rite, a procession of spirits, the scale of a construction site, an extraordinarily present person, or a mythic impossibility can be enough to remind us why Glorantha deserves to be looked at.

The compass that emerges is simple:

> **The world exists without you.**  
> Autonomy, Situation, Forces, Pressures.
>
> **But really look at it.**  
> Focus, Player attention, curiosity, movement of the campaign.
>
> **And when it shows itself, make it live.**  
> Immersion, manifestations, embodied NPCs, Promises, Wow.

The first avoids the rigged scenario. The second avoids railroading. The third prevents the world's autonomy from becoming an excuse for telling stories that are too flat.

## Time's overcorrection and the Rune of Sartar retcon

The session also showed the opposite danger.

After the Player explained in `Off:` that Tal was looking for more meaning and epic breath, Time introduced a recent marginal note into a text: **“Ask the Sages of Boldhome what they still know of the old rites.”**

It was too tailored.

Tal's voluntary research into Sartar could perfectly well lead him to discover traditions concerning the clandestine survival of his cult. But this sentence amounted almost to a **plot hook manufactured on demand**, exactly what the principle of the world's autonomy is meant to avoid.

The consolidation therefore adopts an explicit retcon: the text does not contain that instruction. In its margin, drawn by another hand, Tal discovers only:

## Prepared, emergent, improvised

### Prepared

- Jorstan's death;
- the arrival of the legacy at the Library;
- the family's dispute over the bundle;
- the Forces of the temple, the family, the servants, and time;
- Hendrik's lack of special authority over the legacy;
- the opening of the bundle's contents;
- the possibility, not guaranteed, that Jorstan had left traces.

### Emergent through the Player's choices

- Tal's initial ruse;
- his private judgment of the other Grey Sage;
- his interest in Enastina and Argrathor;
- his growing rejection of the “notary” role;
- his departure from the scene;
- his nostalgia for Grimar and the roads;
- the choice to join the caravan;
- the voluntary search into Jorstan and then Sartar;
- his interest in a clandestine cult of Sartar;
- the desire to see Boldhome one day.

### Improvised by Time

- Enastina as a specific person;
- the details of Argrathor;
- the detailed contents of the bundle and the thick tablet;
- Daran and the news from Alda-Chur, Tarsh, and Furthest;
- Jorstan's archival traces;
- the documents studied about Sartar;
- the first, overly directive version of the marginal note;
- its replacement in consolidation by the Rune of Sartar.

## What the playtest showed

This session is less successful as an initial proposition for play than the previous ones, but it reveals several important properties.

First, **a Situation can fail to hook the Player without the game collapsing**. Because a Situation is not a trajectory, Tal can leave it and the world continues.

Second, the Player's control of the gaze may not operate only inside a resolution through Bets, Focus, and Zoom. It can act at campaign scale: what Tal chooses to observe, pursue, or abandon eventually moves the story itself.

Finally, the autonomy of the world and listening to the table are not opposing principles. Time's challenge is to **take the players' appetite into account without retrospectively manufacturing a world that obeys them**, while making Glorantha embodied, wondrous, and surprising enough that its autonomy remains desirable to explore.
