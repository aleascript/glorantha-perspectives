# Actual Play — first session

This page reconstructs **Talimar's first session, played on August 31, 2026**, in the order in which its decisive elements appeared at the table. It keeps the player's choices, Bets, rolls, rulings, and lines of dialogue that transformed Tal.

The playtest transcript kept in the working notes is longer still. This edition removes repetitions and purely technical turns without retroactively rebuilding a character who did not yet exist.

## 1. Funnel — finding Tal through play

The game begins without a finished sheet. The player chooses to inhabit someone who fully belongs to an Orlanthi community but is no longer satisfied by the world as it is explained to him.

Asked what troubles him, he says he cannot position himself absolutely against the Lunar Empire while being equally unable to condone its customs and deeds. He feels **torn, almost orphaned**.

He explains why. Tal has seen the baths, buildings, statues, clothing, Lunar organization, and a way of exposing several sides of a conflict that contrasts with the roughness of his own culture. Yet the same society has slaves, dominates other peoples, and feeds the Crimson Bat.

A strong truth therefore appears before any mechanics: **Tal refuses to make one of these realities false so the other can become simple.**

The player adds that a merchant uncle often took him into Tarsh. That uncle becomes **Grimar**. In a memory from his youth, Tal marvels at Lunar façades. Grimar tells him:

> “When you admire a great house, always ask yourself who carried the stones.”

The family naturally expected Tal to become a merchant, probably in Issaries' orbit. The player instead discovers that trade bores him. What draws him are myths, histories, differences between accounts, and the possibility of traveling in order to understand.

**Lhankor Mhy appears as a possibility during play, not as a profession chosen before the game.**

## 2. Choosing to play the vocation

The player could immediately decide that Tal is an apprentice or initiate. He explicitly chooses the opposite: he wants to play the path all the way to initiation and wants it tied to a Situation.

The table therefore decides neither who will recruit Tal nor whether he will be found worthy to follow that road. The possibility exists; the story remains open.

A few years pass in the fiction. Tal still travels with Grimar. On a road back from Tarsh, something catches his eye: an old stone recently uncovered from the earth, and beside it an old man copying its marks onto a tablet.

Tal suddenly recognizes that the marks are not decoration.

**They are writing.**

## 3. Situation — the stone beside the road

The player wants a genuine opportunity to make contact with the stranger. He identifies what may weigh on the Situation.

**Tal's Bets:**

- his relationship with Grimar and their many journeys together;
- offering to share salt under Heortling hospitality;
- his curiosity.

**Adversity Bets:**

- the wind is rising and there is still a long road ahead;
- Grimar wants to keep moving.

The resolution is therefore **3 Bets against 2**.

Tal rolls `2, 3, 6`. Under his theist reading, the `6` lets him reroll the odd `3`, which becomes `5`. Final result: `2, 5, 6`, for **2 Successes**.

Adversity rolls `3, 1`: **0 Successes**.

**2–0: dominated result, so a Feat is available.**

Grimar does not merely grant Tal a few moments. Tal earns a real meeting. The old man hands him his tablet and asks:

> **“Tell me what is wrong.”**

Tal finds **seven differences** between the inscription on the stone and the copy. He assumes the tablet is wrong because the stone is older. The man simply asks:

> **“Why not the stone?”**

The stone is older than the tablet, yes. It is not necessarily older than whoever carved it. What Tal actually knows is narrower: **the two texts differ**.

The man introduces himself: **Hendrik, son of Enderos, Grey Sage of Lhankor Mhy.**

Tal replies with his entire genealogy: Talimar, son of Calimar and Urestona, carls of the Grey Heron clan, of the Colymar tribe.

## 4. The meeting becomes an apprenticeship

Tal questions Hendrik about his travels. The Sage has read Lunar texts, spoken to their priests and scholars, traveled through Tarsh, Esrolia, Prax, part of the Holy Country, and met Westerners.

Hendrik notices that Tal first asks **how far** he has gone.

Tal answers:

> “Because that is where I want to go too.”

Hendrik corrects him:

> “If you only want to go far, stay with your uncle. Merchants travel more roads than many sages.”

Then he gives Tal one of the sentences that will define him:

> **“I go wherever a question forces me to go.”**

The player proposes that this meeting lead to an apprenticeship. The table does not jump straight to “Tal becomes an apprentice”: it plays through the family agreement and its consequences. Calimar and Urestona must agree; the family can provide food, clothes, writing materials, and a contribution; Tal will retain seasonal obligations.

Hendrik does not promise to make him a Grey Sage. He promises to teach him to read, write, search, distinguish what he knows from what he believes he knows, and preserve what others have learned — then discover whether he can truly become more.

A few weeks later Tal leaves the Grey Heron. His mother prepares his things. His father gives him an ancestral knife. Grimar gives him a purse and far too much advice. Hendrik gives him the **tablet of the seven differences**.

His first lesson begins with a question:

> **“Where are we going?”**

Hendrik gives no answer.

> **PROCEDURE** — A player's proposal can become fiction immediately, without a resolution, when nothing meaningfully opposes it. Here the point is to play the social and material consequences of apprenticeship, not ask the dice whether the story is allowed to continue.

## 5. The three testimonies

After some time, Hendrik gives the apprentices three contradictory accounts of the same conflict between clans.

Tal refuses to treat one as “the correct text”. He wants to reconstruct a plausible sequence while preserving the possibility that truth remains incomplete.

The player explicitly asks to use resolution: he knows what Tal is trying to achieve but does not want to solve the investigation himself as a puzzle.

**Tal's Bets:**

- Curiosity;
- Hendrik's teaching;
- “Truth may remain incomplete.”

**Adversity Bets:**

- Three contradictory testimonies;
- Every account has its blind spot.

Again: **3 against 2**.

Tal rolls `4, 5, 2`: **2 Successes**.  
Adversity rolls `1, 5`: **0 Successes**.

Another **Feat**.

Tal proposes one reconstruction that accounts for all three testimonies. Then he produces a second, equally compatible one. He refuses to claim which is true.

The consequence of the Feat is not merely “he solves the exercise very well”. Hendrik is genuinely impressed and the other apprentices begin to look at Tal differently. The result leaves a trace in the fiction.

> **PROCEDURE** — The player asked for resolution when the Protagonist's ability needed to take over from his own ability to solve the problem. The Feat is then interpreted as a lasting transformation of the Situation, not merely a more elegant performance.

## 6. The myth of Lhankor Mhy

Hendrik gathers the apprentices and initially forbids them to write:

> “You will write it later. First you need to know why we write.”

He tells of Lhankor Mhy born from **Orenoar, Mistress of Truth**, upon **Mostal, the Mountain of Law**. He mentions traditions that do not all agree about his father, the god's knowledge, Elasa's disappearance into the Darkness, the invention of writing to preserve what had been learned, and then Lhankor Mhy's journey with the Lightbringers.

The essential point for Tal is not a list of powers. Hendrik stresses something else: **Lhankor Mhy sometimes succeeds, and sometimes fails.** Even the god who embraces all knowledge continues searching.

Hendrik recalls an old statement of Tal's: “Only Lhankor Mhy knows.” Then he asks:

> **“Now that you know this story, why do you think he lets us search?”**

Tal answers:

> **“Because he himself, who knows everything, keeps searching. It is his quest; it is our quest.”**

Hendrik does not correct him.

When tablets are finally allowed, Tal does not merely record the events of the myth. He writes how Lhankor Mhy acts:

- distinguish what is true;
- remember what has been learned;
- preserve knowledge through writing;
- seek what is missing;
- continue searching even when one already knows.

These phrases become character truths and perhaps, later, magic. At this point the game does not yet decide what they are “worth” mechanically.

## 7. The Runes Tal thinks he recognizes

Several seasons pass. Hendrik sends the apprentices outside with one instruction:

> **“Seek nothing.”**

When they ask what they should bring back:

> **“What you find.”**

No roll is called for. There is neither opposition nor uncertain Intention to resolve.

Tal climbs as high as he can. His mind has been boiling for months; he wants calm and perspective. The wind reminds him of his clan. He looks at the layers of the world: earth, Air, sky, Yelm, stars, Moon, then what lies below. He feels himself between things.

Back with Hendrik, the player himself proposes three Runes from what has just been lived:

- **Air** — the middle, the interface;
- **Harmony** — what binds opposites without erasing their differences;
- **Movement** — the curiosity that began his quest.

Hendrik asks Tal what he means by Harmony. Tal answers:

> “Things are. Even when contrary, they are there, together, and Harmony is what binds them.”

Air, Harmony, and Movement are written on his tablet.

> **PROCEDURE** — The Runes are proposed by the player after a Situation gives him material through which to recognize them. They are not selected from a list prepared by Time.

## 8. Initiation

A few seasons later, Hendrik presents Tal for initiation into Lhankor Mhy.

At the entrance to the sanctuary, every tablet is taken from him, including the one that bears Air, Harmony, and Movement.

> “You do not take what you know with you. You are about to discover what remains when you cannot consult it.”

Before the Runes of **Truth** and **Law**, a Sage asks:

> **“What do you seek here?”**

Tal answers that he seeks Lhankor Mhy's agreement and support and would devote his life to Law and Truth.

Immediately he doubts himself. Neither Law nor Truth appeared among the Runes he thought he had recognized.

The Sages do not correct him. Instead they ask whether he offers his life to Lhankor Mhy or to the Runes, then invite him to look at his doubt. A man does not need to be the Wind to worship Orlanth; a man who already possessed Truth would probably not come seeking it.

Tal suddenly understands what had escaped him.

What he called **Harmony** was, for him, an intuition of **Law**: what allows different things to belong to the same Cosmos without abolishing their differences.

What he called **Movement** was the manifestation of **Truth**: not an answer possessed, but the direction of his entire quest.

Tal falls to his knees. He no longer knows exactly whether he has succeeded or failed. Everything seems to fit together. He feels outside the world, perhaps in the Gods World.

A grey-bearded presence seems to stand before him amid everything that has been known, forgotten, and remains to be sought.

The first question returns:

> **“Tell me what is wrong.”**

But this time there is no tablet to compare.

There is the world.

Tal returns to himself. The Sages trace **Truth**, then **Law**, on his brow. Hendrik concludes:

> **“Now, seek.”**

> **PROCEDURE** — No initiation roll. The reinterpretation Harmony → Law and Movement → Truth grows from the player's response to the doubt that arose in the scene; it was not prepared as a correction to the character sheet.
