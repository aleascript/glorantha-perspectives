# Time Backstage

:::warning[Spoilers]

This page reveals what Time had prepared and how it reacted during Session 0. Threads that remain secret for future sessions are not published here.

:::

Creating Talimar was not meant to lead an already defined character toward an initiation that had already been decided. **Creation itself was play.**

## Initial intention

When the player revealed a possible interest in **Lhankor Mhy**, Time did not decide that Tal would become a Grey Sage.

The intention was simpler: **put that aspiration into a Situation so that play could confirm it, transform it, or abandon it.**

The stone and the tablet came from that intention. They formed a gentle test: a concrete, bounded problem without spectacular danger, allowing the table to see what truly attracted Tal to knowledge.

The driving question was then:

> **Is there a way for Tal to inhabit the contradictions of the world without prematurely choosing which one to deny?**

Lhankor Mhy could become an answer. He was not to be imposed as one.

## Forces

### Grimar, family, and community

Grimar was not conceived as the obstacle to Tal's “true vocation.” He represented a perfectly viable path: travel, trade, pragmatism, and clan ties.

If Tal chose another road, that choice had to carry a real emotional cost precisely because what he was leaving had value.

### Hendrik and Lhankor Mhy

Hendrik was meant to open a way of searching, not provide the right answers.

His deeper function was to confront Tal with a few disciplines: distinguish facts, interpretations, and beliefs; accept incompleteness; endure “I don't know”; let a question compel him to travel.

### The contradictory world

Orlanthi and Lunars, clan and Empire, tradition and discovery were not an opposition to be resolved. They formed the field of tensions within which Tal was looking for his place.

## Pressures present at the start

- The family could naturally expect Tal to continue along Grimar's mercantile path.
- Tal felt a real attraction toward some aspects of Lunar civilization and an equally real revulsion toward its domination.
- His curiosity still had to become a vocation — or reveal that it was not one.
- Seeking truth could harden into a new certainty; Hendrik had to keep open the distinction between **seeking** Truth and **possessing** it.

## What was prepared

Only three elements were truly intentional once the possibility of Lhankor Mhy appeared:

1. **bring about an encounter with a scholar** who might open that path without guaranteeing it;
2. use the **stone and tablet** as an epistemological problem: move from “find the error” to “how do you know which version is false?”;
3. let Tal discover Lhankor Mhy through **Situations, teachings, and myths**, rather than through a catalogue of powers or abilities.

Everything else had to remain available to be produced by the player's answers and the consequences of the fiction.

## What play made emerge

Much of what now defines Tal was therefore not prepared.

**Grimar** gained an unexpected emotional depth. He could have remained merely a practical merchant uncle; play made him a genuine root for Tal, capable both of showing him the world and of letting him leave.

The feeling of **calm before a bounded problem** appeared directly in the player's response to the stone and tablet. It became a major key to the character: Tal does not seek knowledge only out of ambition; he finds relief when the noise of the world can be looked at precisely.

The precise temperament of **Hendrik** was largely improvised in response to Tal: the accusation of Western sorcery, the endless genealogy, the questions about his travels, the desire to go far away. His irony and teaching style grew from those exchanges.

The **Feat of the three testimonies** had not been prepared as a moment of social recognition. Time's first treatment of it was in fact too weak. The player pointed out that a Feat should leave a stronger mark. The consequence was corrected at the table: Tal produces two compatible reconstructions, refuses to claim he knows which one is true, impresses Hendrik, and changes how the other apprentices look at him.

Finally, the Runes emerged entirely from play:

- **Air** as middle and interface, not merely as an Orlanthi temperament;
- **Harmony** as Tal's personal attempt to name what binds contrary realities;
- **Movement** as curiosity and the need to go and see;
- then, during initiation, the realization that for Tal **Harmony expressed Law at a deeper level** and that **Movement was oriented toward Truth**.

This last transformation was not a prepared result. It arose from the player's doubt when he realized that Tal had just offered his life to Law and Truth even though he had not recognized either among his own Runes.

The return of **“Tell me what's wrong”** during the initiation experience was likewise an improvised reuse of something play had made significant.

## How Time reacted

The logic throughout the session was to pick up what the player made important rather than bring Tal back toward a predetermined trajectory.

When Tal became interested in Hendrik's travels, the apprenticeship began to take the form of a quest driven by questions. When the stone calmed him, his training placed more emphasis on distinctions and facts. When he proposed Air, Harmony, and Movement, Hendrik did not correct the sheet: he asked Tal to clarify what those words meant to him. And when initiation suddenly made Law and Truth central, the scene used that discrepancy instead of treating it as a character-creation error.

## What the playtest showed about GP

The clearest loop in the session was:

> **lived fiction → truths about the Protagonist → new possible Bets → new fiction**

The sheet behaved less like a prior definition of the character and more like a **structured memory of what play had discovered about him**.

The session also showed that a resolution result must produce a consequence commensurate with its margin. The Feat of the three testimonies led to a working principle: the scope of a result does not measure only the technical quality of success, but **what it now makes true in the fiction**.

Finally, religious apprenticeship suggested a particularly fruitful sequence for GP:

> **myth → personal understanding → keywords → imitation → possibly magic**

The myth told by Hendrik drew on published Lhankor Mhy lore, notably *Hero Wars — Storm Tribe*, then was condensed and adapted as the character's oral teaching. The source material and the particular way Hendrik tells it are therefore not the same thing.
