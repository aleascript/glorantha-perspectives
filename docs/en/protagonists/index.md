# Protagonists

A Protagonist in **Glorantha Perspectives** is not a combination of characteristics, skills, and powers. They are a person caught up in a world: belongings, relationships, obligations, Runes, myths, injuries, resources, and contradictions may all matter when they act.

Creation is therefore not about filling in a grid. It is about bringing enough meaningful things into view to begin play — then letting the fiction reveal, transform, and sometimes contradict what everyone thought they knew.

The sheet becomes less a form than a **Living Portrait**: the memory of what play has brought into view about the Protagonist.

## The Living Portrait

A Living Portrait may be very short or highly developed. Its form remains open, but five elements often recur.

### Name, image, voice

Begin with what lets you immediately see someone:

- their **name**;
- their **age**, if it matters;
- perhaps an **illustration**;
- a **typical phrase**, conviction, or few words that give them a voice.

The phrase is not a rule. It simply helps you quickly recover how the Protagonist looks at the world.

### The world they inhabit

A Protagonist is not defined only by what they can do. They inhabit a Cosmos and maintain relationships with some of its powers that may become operative.

Bring into view early:

- their **Worldview** — Theism, Animism, Logic, Mysticism, Draconic Thought, or another Perspective they genuinely inhabit;
- the cults, traditions, schools, paths, or sacred communities to which they belong;
- the initiations, oaths, pacts, teachings, or relationships that genuinely bind them;
- the myths, rites, formulas, tradition stories, or spiritual encounters that shape what they can recognize and accomplish.

> **Knowing is not yet participating.**

A theist may know a myth without being able to extend its gestures magically. Initiation establishes a relationship with the God; the myth then provides keys for recognizing how that God acts and how a mortal gesture may enter into continuity with the divine one.

A novice may have received only part of those teachings. An animist instead maintains concrete relationships with spirits, fetishes, and their taboos. A Logician may be trained by a school and its formulas; a mystic by a discipline and a path. The form changes with the Perspective, but the Portrait should show **what actually connects this person to the Cosmos**, not merely what they have heard about it.

### Runes

Usually choose two or three Runes that strongly mark the Protagonist.

A Rune is not merely a domain of action. Ask instead:

> **How does this Rune exist in this person?**

The same Rune can therefore take very different forms from one person to another. **Air** may be anger and impulsiveness, but also the feeling of inhabiting the space between things. **Truth** may be certainty, frankness, inquiry, or the inability to stop searching.

Under each Rune, note a few personal expressions: character, gestures, perceptions, affinities, tensions, or ways of acting. A Rune becomes a Bet only when it genuinely matters in the Situation.

### Life Chapters

The heart of the Living Portrait is made of **Life Chapters**.

A Life Chapter is simply a block that names something important in the Protagonist's existence. It may be a culture, community, profession, cult, tradition, relationship, object, myth, social role, or any other aspect of their life important enough to deserve its own space.

For example:

- **Guardian of Jillaro**;
- **Sand Nomad**;
- **Imperial Shadow**;
- **Initiate of the Great Mystery**;
- **Fta-Ah, my antelope**;
- **The knife my father gave me**;
- **The tradition that must not die**.

Within a Chapter, freely record what belongs there: knowledge, talents, obligations, virtues, relationships, objects, magic, reputation, enemies, taboos, memories, or resources.

There is no mandatory list of Chapters. Two Protagonists from the same culture may organize their Portraits very differently because the same things do not matter to them in the same way.

### Traces

Play leaves traces.

An injury, debt, initiation, reputation, oath, discovery, disgrace, lost object, new relationship, or different understanding of a myth may lastingly change the Portrait.

These elements may be grouped under **Traces**, or integrated directly into the Life Chapter they transform.

The Portrait is not meant to remain stable. When it changes, it is usually because the Protagonist has changed.

## What matters to them?

Before looking for details, ask a few simple questions:

- **Who do they belong with?** What community gives them a place, duties, and support?
- **What world do they believe they live in?** What gods, spirits, laws, traditions, or principles shape their Perspective?
- **What are they genuinely connected to?** What initiations, affiliations, spiritual relationships, schools, or paths make certain things operative for them?
- **What gestures of the sacred have they learned to recognize?** What myths, rites, teachings, or stories have changed the way they act?
- **What Runes mark them?** What do those Runes say about their character, affinities, and contradictions?
- **Who are they bound to?** Whom do they love or hate, and to whom do they owe a debt, oath, or responsibility?
- **What sets them apart?** What talent, flaw, object, status, knowledge, or memory is likely to return in play?
- **What could move them to act?** What do they want to protect, gain, repair, understand, or refuse?

You do not need to answer everything before play begins. These questions direct attention; Situations will reveal what actually deserves to be developed.

## Magic, relationships, and resources

There is no need to create separate sections for magic, relationships, or equipment.

Place them where they make sense — and for magic, preserve as much as possible **the relationship that gives it its reach**.

A **mercury armour** may belong under **Guardian of Jillaro**. A mythic teaching may remain within the Chapter of the cult that transmitted it. A fetish and its taboo may remain attached to a **Tradition of Waha**. A mount may be only one element of **Sand Nomad**; for Peek, **Fta-Ah** deserves a Chapter of her own because their relationship is central. The level of detail depends on the place an element occupies in this life, not on its category.

A magical effect isolated from its source quickly starts to look like an abstract ability. An embodied myth, a spirit with which a relationship exists, a formula received from a school, or a practice acquired through a path instead explains **why this magic exists in this person**.

This organization avoids reducing every life to the same anatomy of a character sheet.

## When should you stop?

Stop creation when you can answer these questions:

1. **Who are they?**
2. **Who do they belong with?**
3. **How do they think the world works?**
4. **What are they genuinely connected to in that world?**
5. **What matters deeply to them?**
6. **What can help them or complicate their life in the first Situation?**

The rest can appear during play.

## Creating through play

Not every answer has to exist before the first Situation.

An encounter, a played memory, an initiation, a failure, a myth heard, or a moral decision may bring something into view that deserves to enter the Portrait afterwards.

[**Talimar's Road**](../narratives/talimar-road/) shows this continuity in play. Tal begins with a belonging, contradictions, and questions. His relationship with Lhankor Mhy, his Runes, and his understanding of himself become clearer through the fiction.

Creation, development, and transformation can therefore belong to a single continuity:

> **lived fiction → self-discovery → inscribed elements → new possible Bets → new fiction**

## Changing a Protagonist

Development remains diegetic. When something changes lastingly in the fiction, change the Portrait:

- add a new Chapter;
- remove or transform an element that no longer describes them;
- transform a Rune understood differently;
- record an injury or debt;
- change a relationship;
- gain or lose an object, secret, or responsibility;
- integrate a myth, rite, teaching, or spiritual relationship that has become operative;
- change an affiliation or conviction after a major transformation.

There is no need to wait for the end of an adventure or for points to be awarded.

An experienced Protagonist is not merely someone who possesses more capabilities. They carry more history that may return into the present.
