# Protagonists

A Protagonist is described by what is true of them in Glorantha: their communities, relationships, obligations, beliefs, injuries, resources, magic, and anything that may become important when they act.

There is no list of numerical characteristics to fill in. Creation means establishing enough truths to know **who this Protagonist is, what they want, and what may weigh on their Situations**.

## Identity

Begin with a few simple elements:

- **name**;
- **age**, if it matters;
- a **typical phrase** or short quote that immediately gives them a voice.

The phrase is not a rule. It simply helps you quickly recover how the Protagonist looks at the world.

## Origins and community

Choose their culture and the concrete community they belong to: clan, tribe, city, temple, regiment, school, family, tradition, or another relevant group.

A community brings support, duties, relationships, enemies, and habits. Any of them may become Bets when they are genuinely present in the Situation.

## Worldview

Choose the deep way the Protagonist understands reality: their **Worldview**.

- **Theism**;
- **Animism**;
- **Logic**;
- **Mysticism**;
- **Draconic Thought**, for beings concerned by it.

Add the spiritual or intellectual affiliation that gives this Worldview a more precise form: cult, tradition, school, path, and so on.

A Protagonist may know rites, texts, practices, or knowledge from elsewhere without changing Worldview. These elements may become Bets; they do not automatically change how the Protagonist's dice are read.

## Runes

Usually choose two or three Runes that strongly mark the Protagonist.

They may express:

- character;
- a magical affinity;
- a way of acting;
- a cultural or religious mark;
- an important contradiction.

A Rune is not an automatic bonus. It becomes a Bet when it genuinely matters in the Situation.

For example, a strong affinity with **Air** may support a character's impulsiveness, pride, or violence; **Illusion** may support secrecy, deception, or the play of appearances. The same truths can also turn against them.

## Keywords and traits

Add a free list of important things:

- talents;
- flaws;
- habits;
- physical features;
- remarkable objects;
- reputation;
- knowledge;
- responsibilities.

Prefer wording that immediately evokes a person and a world.

**Disillusioned veteran of the Sartar Rebellion** gives more to play with than **Warrior**. **Broadsword inherited from his father** gives more than **Good weapon**.

## State

Record states that deserve to remain true from one Situation to the next:

- injury;
- exhaustion;
- disturbance;
- lack of resources;
- transformation;
- lasting favor or disgrace.

A state does not need a numerical scale to matter. If it genuinely affects what happens, it may become a Bet or change the frame of the Situation.

## Bonds and attachments

Record what strongly connects the Protagonist to others:

- a loved one;
- a rival;
- a debt;
- an enemy;
- an oath;
- responsibility toward a community;
- a relationship with a spirit, god, place, or object.

Bonds give reasons to act and give the world ways to answer.

## Magic and resources

Record what the Protagonist can genuinely do or call upon in the world: feats, spirits, spells, grimoires, secrets, objects, allies, wealth, mounts, status, or another important resource.

You do not need to list everything a person from that culture would normally know how to do. Record mainly what distinguishes this Protagonist or is likely to matter often.

## When should you stop?

Stop creation when you can answer these questions:

1. **Who are they?**
2. **Who do they belong with?**
3. **How do they think the world works?**
4. **What matters deeply to them?**
5. **What can help them or complicate their life in the first Situation?**

The rest can appear during play. A truth discovered later can be added when it becomes important.

## Creating through play

Not every answer has to exist before the first Situation.

You can begin with a few truths solid enough to play, then discover the rest through choices, encounters, myths, and consequences. The sheet then becomes the memory of what play has actually revealed about the Protagonist.

[**Talimar's Road**](../narratives/talimar-road/) shows this possibility in play: Tal begins with a community, contradictions, and questions, while his relationship with Lhankor Mhy, his Runes, and his initiation become clearer through the fiction.

## Changing a Protagonist

Development remains diegetic. When something changes lastingly in the fiction, change the description:

- add a new bond;
- remove a trait that is no longer true;
- transform a reputation;
- record an injury;
- gain an object, secret, or responsibility;
- change an affiliation or conviction after a major transformation.

There is no need to wait for the end of an adventure or for points to be awarded.
