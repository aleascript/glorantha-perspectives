# Examples of Protagonists

These examples show a level of detail that is sufficient for play. They are not mandatory templates.

## Varmand “Spearbreaker”

**Age:** 24  
**Phrase:** “The storm does not ask permission to blow!”

**Origins:** Sartarite, Apple Clan.  
**Worldview:** Theist, initiate of Orlanth Adventurous.

**Runes:**

- **Air**: impulsive, hot-tempered, loud;
- **Movement**: restless, dislikes confinement;
- **Death**: merciless toward the enemies of his clan.

**Keywords and traits:** raid tactician; broadsword inherited from his father; voice that carries over the din; cannot lie.

**Bonds and attachments:** wants revenge for his brother killed by the Lunars; secretly in love with the priestess of a neighboring clan.

### In play

Varmand tries to rally hesitant warriors before a raid.

His **carrying voice** may matter. His affinity with **Air** may support his passion. His **inherited sword** may become a symbol of legitimacy if the warriors know its story.

These three elements do not automatically count as three Stakes in every scene. Here they may matter because the Situation makes them relevant.

## Centurion Phargos

A character carried by Time can be much lighter.

**Worldview:** Lunar theist.  
**Goal:** maintain order and collect taxes.

**Keywords:** disciplined veteran; phalanx training; heavy hoplite armor; imperial arrogance.

Phargos does not need a complete description before entering play. If the Situation makes him important, his relationships, Runes, obligations, or injuries can be defined later.

## Variable detail

The right amount of detail depends on what the game follows.

A lasting Protagonist usually deserves an identity, a community, a worldview, a few Runes, bonds, and several traits. Someone encountered for a few minutes may fit into one intention and two strong truths.

The principle remains the same: **describe what can become important in the fiction, rather than filling out a grid for its own sake.**
