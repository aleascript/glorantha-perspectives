# A worldview is not a mask

In **Glorantha Perspectives**, a **worldview** represents neither a profession, an intellectual method, nor the kind of magic a character happens to be using. Above all, it represents their **lived ontology**: the deep and spontaneous way in which they think the world works.

> **The worldview does not describe what you do. It describes the world in which you think you are doing it.**

This distinction is essential in Glorantha, because characters from very different traditions can know the same facts, use comparable methods, or even learn one another's practices without inhabiting the same world.

## Knowing is not inhabiting

A Grey Sage of Lhankor Mhy may study a Malkioni treatise, understand a sorcerous argument, and reproduce a foreign procedure. This does not turn them into a Logician. They normally remain a **Theist**: the Gods, the Runes, and mythical events are still operative realities of the Cosmos for them.

Likewise, astrologers of **Buserian** and scholars of **Irripi Ontor** may observe, calculate, classify, and reason with great rigor without belonging to the Logic worldview. Their scholarship operates inside a theist world.

A Buserian may calculate the movement of a star because that star is also a divine power whose course can be known. A Malkioni may perform a comparable calculation because they understand the Cosmos as intelligible through Law and Reason. **The calculation may be the same; the world in which it makes sense is not.**

This is why Logic is not "the worldview of intelligent people," scholars, or rational characters. The **Malkioni** — and, in another way, the **Mostali** — normally belong to Logic because that structure corresponds to their conception of the Cosmos.

## Learning a foreign practice does not change the worldview

Cultures circulate, trade, fight, and influence one another. They therefore borrow rites, techniques, and bodies of knowledge.

The **Pol-Joni** provide a particularly clear example. They live in Prax and have adopted animist customs and rituals necessary to integrate into that environment. Yet they do not cease to be fundamentally Orlanthi: their usual worldview remains **Theism**.

Conversely, knowing another culture's rite does not mean adopting its ontology. A Malkioni may learn the gestures and words of an Orlanthi rite without becoming a Theist for the duration of the ceremony. A shaman may know the cults of their neighbors perfectly well without ceasing to be an Animist.

## Foreign knowledge becomes Bets

These forms of learning still matter in the fiction. A Malkioni grimoire, a Buserian calendar, a Praxian ritual, a Mostali instrument, the teaching of a foreign master, or a learned language can become **Bets** whenever they are relevant to the situation.

The mechanical distinction is therefore simple:

- **what the character knows, owns, practices, or has learned feeds their Bets;**
- **what they believe the world to be determines their worldview.**

There is no need to choose the "best" worldview for a scene. The character resolves the situation from the world they inhabit, while freely drawing on whatever knowledge their history has given them.

## Some landmarks

| Culture or tradition | Usual worldview | What may vary without changing the worldview |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sartarite / Heortling | Theism | Foreign knowledge, texts, and practices |
| Grey Sage of Lhankor Mhy | Theism | Scholarly methods, foreign texts and grimoires |
| Astrologer of Buserian | Theism | Calculations, observations, and astronomical corpora |
| Scholar of Irripi Ontor | Theism | Calculations, classifications, and foreign knowledge |
| Pol-Joni | Theism | Praxian rites and practices |
| Kolati | Animism | Knowledge of the surrounding theist culture |
| Traditional Praxian | Animism | Foreign cults and practices according to experience |
| Malkioni | Logic | Foreign practices learned without conversion |
| Mostali | Logic | Techniques and interactions with other traditions |

These indications describe common cases, not cultural prisons. Glorantha allows conversions, revelations, and profound transformations.

## Truly changing worldview

A change of worldview is therefore possible, but it should not be treated as changing tools. Learning a skill, reading a new book, or mastering a rite is not enough.

An event must truly transform the character's relationship to the Cosmos: a deep conversion, an initiation that overturns their understanding of reality, an irreversible mystical experience, a draconic transformation, or another comparable shift in the fiction.

Changing worldview does not mean deciding to look at the world differently for a moment.

**It means discovering that you can no longer look at it as you did before.**

This Gloranthan conception is one of the origins of the more general notion of a **Prism** in *Resonance*. In *Glorantha Perspectives*, we deliberately keep the term **worldview**.
