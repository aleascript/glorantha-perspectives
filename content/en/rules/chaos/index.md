> "Chaos is not strong because it has better weapons. Chaos is strong because it does not play the same game as we do."
>
> — An initiate of the Storm Bull, before charging a horde of broos.

Chaos is the force of entropy and of the destruction of the Cosmos. Mechanically, consorting with Chaos (willingly or out of despair) allows one to break the fundamental rules of the game, but the price to pay is a permanent physical or spiritual corruption: the Taint.

## The Chaotic Temptation (The Cheat)

When a character faces certain death, a bitter failure, or is blocked by a [Frame Factor](../stakes) (such as the stasis of a temple), the player may invoke the force of Chaos to get out of it. Narratively, this translates into an unnatural act (absorbing the energy of an impious artifact, sacrificing an ally, or letting the corruption express itself).

Mechanically, the player grants themselves an absolute advantage that violates the resolution engine:

- They can *instantly convert* any die of their roll into a critical success (a 6, an even number, etc., depending on their frame).
- Or they can *completely ignore a Frame Factor* to impose their own Stakes.

## The Counterpart: The Taint

Chaos always gives, but it corrupts everything it touches. In exchange for this cosmic cheat, the character gains a Taint Keyword (e.g., _Tentacular arm_, _Pus-covered skin_, _Thirst for fresh flesh_, _Voice from beyond the grave_).

## The Evolution of the Taint

The Taint is not a fixed mark: it is a living Chaos Keyword that evolves at the pace of its use. Each use of Chaos can make an existing Taint mutate rather than create a new one. Destiny makes the Keyword evolve in stages, from the most discreet sign to the most monstrous:

- **The first sign**: the Taint is discreet, almost benign, but already against nature. *Example: a strange lump, a passing craving.*
- **The sign asserts itself**: the Taint becomes visible and constraining. *Example: the lump opens into a small tentacle; the craving becomes a thirst for human blood once a month.*
- **The hold grows**: the Taint invades daily life. *Example: the tentacle grows; the thirst becomes weekly, then daily.*
- **Total hold**: the Taint leaves no room for the human anymore. *Example: the mutation spreads across the whole body; the thirst becomes permanent and unquenchable.*

Each stage strengthens the Keyword: Destiny uses it as an additional Stake for the adversity, and its new intensity lends itself to new Stakes in conflicts involving nature, the gods or healthy society.

## The Weight of Chaos (Mechanic of the Taint)

Unlike the character's other Keywords, a Taint can *never* be used by the player to generate a positive Stake in their favor. The Cosmos viscerally rejects Chaos.

- **The Hostility of the World**: from now on, each time the character undertakes an action involving nature, the gods, or healthy society, Destiny (the gamemaster) uses this Taint Keyword *automatically* as an *additional Stake for the adversity*.
- **The Spiral**: the more a character cheats using Chaos, the more Taints they accumulate, constantly increasing the number of Stakes against them in their daily life, until they can no longer survive within the Cosmos and become an unplayable monster.

## Notes: The Chaotic Contribution of Frames of Thought

Reflections on the share of Chaos that each [frame of thought](../) carries within it.

- **Theism**: it believes it can contain Chaos, but that didn't work. It is likely that the explosion of the Six is partly responsible through **divine explosion**. Hence the mess in the mythic ages and the Great Compromise. The Gods are the main source of Chaos.
- **Mysticism**: it is not its subject. It even tends to make entropic sources (the Six) disappear through its **transformations**. If everyone were mystical, the very notion of Chaos would not exist.
- **Animism**: it manipulates **nature**, it does not innovate. New developments are exceedingly rare. If everyone were animist, Chaos would not exist.
- **Logic**: it does not carry Chaos within itself, but can produce results that seem chaotic through the magnitude of certain outcomes: it therefore brings novelty on a grand scale through **effects**.
- **The cultists of Chaos**: essentially theists (or even animists), they need no additional rules. The Chaotic Gods appeared because there were breaches, but they respect the usual rules as Gods (Great Compromise, etc.).

> Chaos must remain a particular philosophical vision: a perspective on change and novelty, and on what is against nature and what perverts it.
